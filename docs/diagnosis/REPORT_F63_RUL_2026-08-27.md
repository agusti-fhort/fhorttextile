## 0 · The verdict

1. ✅ **The RUL reproduces Montse's nest exactly, and that closes the phase question.** Base
   size + the table + the map, applied to the cut contour of all five pieces at all four
   non-base sizes: **7 376 points, mean 0,0018 mm, worst 0,0073 mm, 100 % inside the 0,5 mm
   criterion.** The criterion had been missed by 6,18 mm for two sprints. It is not missed by
   a factor of 12; it is met by a factor of 68.
2. 🔑 **And the residual is not an achievement, it is the file's own rounding.** The RUL gives
   deltas to two decimals, so a *correct* map cannot do better than 0,0071 mm on the diagonal.
   We measure 0,0073. There is nothing left in that column to improve.
3. 🚨 **The map is not the identity, and finding that out was the sprint's real risk.** The
   geometry invokes rules 1 and 65–98, 171–198, 226–238; the table declares 1–90. A `# 65` is
   **not** `RULE: DELTA 65`. The correspondence is structural (per-piece contiguous blocks, in
   order) and gives three constant offsets — 49, 121, 148 — that the code *derives* and never
   writes down. §2.
4. 🚨 **The RUL refutes the structure that F6.1 and F6.2 both assumed, twice over.** Every turn
   point carries its **own** rule — so there is no grouping of points to declare, which is what
   the brief expected to hand the solver. And a single point's four displacements **are not
   collinear**: fitting one direction per point leaves 3,0 mm rms and 7,2 mm at worst. §5.
5. 🔑 **The reason is a fact about the garment, and it is the sprint's finding.** X and Y grade
   on **different schedules**. On the body, width goes `0 · 1 · 2 · 2,5` and length goes
   `−0,67 · 1 · 2 · 3`; on the sleeve, width goes to 3. One amplitude per size cannot produce
   both — which is exactly why F6.2's rank 1 was refuted and its rank 2 had zero leak, and why
   neither moved the vertex metric. §5.
6. **There is no nesting component to separate.** The brief expected a modal X per group that
   was the nest translation and had to be removed before comparing. Measured: removing anything
   would have *introduced* error. The X deltas are grading. §1.4.
7. ❌ **The solver given the declared schedule does NOT close the vertex gap — it widens it**
   (6,18 → 23,47 mm), and the report does not dress that up. But the cause is measured and it
   is not the schedule: Montse's own field is **inside** the declared model to 0,52 mm rms and
   satisfies the targets to 0,51 mm. The search is what fails — coupling leaves 85 free degrees
   of freedom on the back and the regulariser has no opinion about field magnitude (`bend` is
   zero on affine fields, `stretch` defaults to 0). §4.3.
8. 🔑 **Which no longer matters for the phase, and that is the point of clause 1.** The vertex
   question was «can the motor infer a pattern-maker's per-point decisions from 16
   measurements». The answer is still no, from a fourth direction. The RUL makes the question
   unnecessary where it exists.

## 1 · FASE A — the file, and the five checks

`engine/rul_reader.py` reads it. **No second parser was written**, and that is a decision worth
naming: the reader has been in the product since S1, its docstring already predicted this file
(«the values will arrive with a genuinely populated RUL»), and a bench copy of the grammar
would mean a regression in the product reader could not be seen here. What `ops/rosetta/rul_837.py`
adds is what a reader cannot know: the checks on the **values**, and the map to the geometry.

| | check | verdict | evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | the rules are contiguous | ✅ | 90 rules, 1–90, no gaps |
| A2 | the base size does not move | ✅ | S is `(0,0)` in all 90 |
| A3 | max \|dy\| per size is what the brief measured | ✅ | XS 10,61 · M 15,15 · L 30,12 · XL 45,35 |
| A4 | the 12 rules of most movement share **one** progression | ❌ | they do not — see below |
| A5 | the reader denounces nothing | ✅ | no issues; METRIC, 1,0 mm/unit; base `S`; XS S M L XL |

⚠️ **A3 is checked at the brief's own precision, and that is not a loophole.** The brief gave
its bounds to one decimal (10,6 · 15,2 · 30,1 · 45,4). Comparing at more would turn the brief's
rounding into a defect of the file — 10,61 would «exceed» 10,6 and the check would fail with
nothing wrong. It is compared to one decimal, which is all it claims, and it still bites if a
value actually moves.

### 1.4 · A4 is a real failure, and it is the finding

The brief expected one progression, `−0,67 / 1 / 2 / 3`, at the twelve rules of most movement.
There are **two**, and they belong to different axes:

| axis | progressions found at those 12 rules |
|---|---|
| **X** | `(0 · 1 · 2 · 2,5)` — and variants at 2,48 / 2,52 / 2,54 |
| **Y** | `(−0,67 · 1 · 2 · 3)` — and `(−0,70 …)`, `(−1,00 …)` |

They differ at **both ends**: X does not move at all between XS and S where Y moves −2/3, and
X stops at 2,5 at XL where Y goes to 3. Neither gap is rounding. This is the fact everything
else in the report follows from.

**And there is no nest translation to separate.** The brief asked for the modal X component to
be identified as nesting and removed per group before any comparison. It was identified — it is
`0 · 1 · 2 · 2,5` on the body and `0 · 1 · 2 · 3` on the sleeve — and it is **grading, not
nesting**: the reconstruction of §3 applies the deltas *whole*, subtracts nothing, and lands on
Montse's nest at 0,007 mm. Removing a «nesting» component would have moved the answer away from
the measurement. (The bank's own alignment already said so and was not believed until now:
`alineacio.translacio` is `[0,0]` at every size.)

## 2 · FASE B — the map, and why it was the risk

The brief's B1 said: if `PatternFile#20` does not carry the `# n` texts, stop and ask for
another export. **It carries them** — 338 of them, and `aama_reader` has been reading them into
`PointData.grade_rule` since S1. B2's sanity contrast passes exactly: 28 distinct on the front,
24 on the back, 9 on the sleeve, a single shared «1» on collar and placket — the same four
numbers F6.2 measured independently.

🚨 **But the two numberings are not the same.** The geometry invokes 238 distinct numbers (the
sewing line is numbered in threes: 3, 6, 9 … on the front) and the table declares 90. Taking
`# n` for `RULE: DELTA n` would have produced a confident, wrong answer.

The correspondence is structural, and every part of it is checkable:

- each graded piece occupies a **contiguous block** of DXF numbers once its notches are
  included — front 65–98 (34), back 171–198 (28), sleeve 226–238 (13), 75 numbers with no gaps;
- the table's **last 75 rules**, 16–90, split into blocks of exactly those sizes, in order;
- rule **1 is the null rule** (zero at every size) and is what the two pieces that do not grade
  carry.

That yields offsets **+49 / +121 / +148**, which `construeix_mapa` derives from the blocks and
never writes down; if a block had a gap, or the null rule were not unique, or the offset were
not constant across a block, it raises instead of guessing.

| | check | verdict | evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| B2 | per-piece counts are F6.2's | ✅ | 28 · 24 · 9 · 1 · 1 |
| B3 | one rule per turn point, none shared | ✅ | 28/28, 24/24, 9/9 — **no grouping is declared** |
| B4 | every rule the geometry invokes exists | ✅ | 75 mapped to 16–90, plus the null rule 1 |
| B5 | the table declares no orphan rules | ❌ | **14 do**: rules 2–15, never invoked |

🚩 **B5 is reported, not explained away.** Rules 2–15 have zero deltas and no point of the five
pieces asks for them. The map is not what is wrong with them — the map is verified at 0,007 mm
in §3. The likeliest reading is that the table belongs to the **style** and this DXF carries
five of its pieces; that is a question for Montse, not an inference for us.

**The map is not believed on its structure.** §3 is the test, and the test has a control:
shifting the map by **one single position** takes the reconstruction from 0,0018 mm mean /
0,0073 max to **3,74 mm mean / 51,53 max**. A correspondence that survives that is pinned, not
fitted.

### 2.1 · The checks were seen red

Per `ops/rosetta/README.md`, a verification that cannot fail is decoration. All four were made
to fail on purpose, by `dataclasses.replace` on the table or the map before calling them:

| perturbation | check | |
|---|---|---|
| rule 45 removed from the table | A1 | FAIL ✅ |
| S set to 0,01 mm on rule 30 | A2 | FAIL ✅ |
| one `dy` inflated to 60 mm | A3 | FAIL ✅ |
| the whole map shifted by one | B4 | FAIL ✅ |

### 4.3 · 🚨 And the reason is the solver, not the schedule — measured, not argued

It would be easy and wrong to read §4.2 as «the RUL's schedule is not the missing information».
Two measurements say otherwise, and they are the same instrument F6.1 used in its §5.4a:

| | front | back | sleeve |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Montse's own field against the **fitxa's constraints**, worst | 0,513 | 0,361 | 3,007 |
| Montse's own field against the **declared schedule**, rms / max | 0,517 / 2,31 | 0,516 / 2,10 | 0,465 / 1,22 |

**The right answer is inside the declared model, to about half a millimetre**, and it satisfies
the targets. The model is not what fails. What fails is the search:

- coupling collapses the rows. On the back, 28 constraint rows come back **rank 11 over 96
  unknowns — 85 free degrees of freedom** (the L and XL copies of every gauge row and of G1,
  EK2, E1 and SF are named redundant, correctly: under a shared field they say the same thing).
- and **nothing in the regulariser pins those 85.** `bend` is the dominant term and it is
  *zero* on a field that is affine in arc length; `stretch` defaults to 0,0 and `ridge` to
  10⁻⁸, which is conditioning, not a preference. A large affine coefficient field is therefore
  free.
- so the solver returns a **feasible answer that is far away**: on the back the field reaches
  715 mm where Montse's reaches 61 and the per-size solve reaches 27 — with the contract held
  to 2·10⁻¹¹ mm and a leak of 0,53 mm. A `recta` target is a **distance**; a large enough shear
  keeps the distance and moves both endpoints anywhere.

🔑 **The sleeve is the control.** It is the piece with the fewest targets and the least row
collapse, and it is the one where the declared mode changes nothing at all: 7,079 → 7,087.
Where the null space is small the mode is neutral; where it is large the mode is unpinned.

**So D's honest verdict is: not refuted, not demonstrated.** The declared schedule is a correct
description of the pattern (§3, §5.3) and the vertex metric cannot be used to judge it until
the regulariser has an opinion about field magnitude. That is a bounded piece of work — a
non-zero `stretch`, or a norm penalty on the coefficients — and it is named in §7 rather than
attempted here, because inventing a regulariser to make a number look better is exactly the
kind of thing this bench exists to prevent.

## 5 · What the RUL refutes — and it is what both previous sprints assumed

This section is the reason F6.1 and F6.2 could not close the gap, and it is readable straight
off the file. Two refutations, in order of how much they cost.

### 5.1 · There is no grouping of points to declare

The brief's D asked for a solver mode where «points of one rule share the group's direction».
**No two points share a rule.** 28 distinct rules on 28 front turn points, 24 on 24, 9 on 9
(check B3). PolyPattern declares grading **per point**; the near-rank-2 structure F6.2 measured
in the field is *emergent*, and there was never a declared grouping to hand the solver.

What the rules do share is **values**: 26 distinct delta vectors among the front's 34 rules,
24 among the back's 28, 9 among the sleeve's 13 — mirrored points and pairs. That is a property
of the answer, not a structure the pattern declares in advance, and it is not usable as prior
information.

### 5.2 · 🚨 A single point's grading is not rank one

This is the sharp one. Take one rule — one point — and its four displacements. If grading were
«each point moves along its own line, scaled per size», those four vectors would be collinear.
They are not:

| piece | rules with movement | rank-1 residual rms | worst |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| DELANTERO | 31 | **2,99 mm** | 7,14 mm (rule 22) |
| ESPALDA | 27 | **3,14 mm** | 7,15 mm (rule 70) |
| MANGA | 13 | **1,61 mm** | 2,37 mm (rule 80) |

F6.2's rank-1 mode — one direction per point, one amplitude per size — **cannot express even a
single point of this pattern**, let alone a piece. Its own diagnosis had reached the same wall
from the other side («two LINEAR POMs on one piece asking for different XS/M ratios cannot both
be met»), and treated it as a property of the *fitxa's targets*. It is not. It is a property of
the **garment**.

### 5.3 · The law that replaces it: a schedule per axis

Fit each axis separately — `dx_i(t) = ax(t)·u_i`, `dy_i(t) = ay(t)·v_i` — and the same data
that refuses one direction per point falls into place:

| piece | axis | XS | M | L | XL | separable residual rms / max |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| DELANTERO | x | −0,007 | 1,000 | 2,004 | **2,524** | 0,448 / 1,298 mm |
| DELANTERO | y | **−0,671** | 1,000 | 2,001 | 2,996 | 0,334 / 2,294 mm |
| ESPALDA | x | −0,002 | 1,000 | 1,997 | **2,505** | 0,334 / 1,190 mm |
| ESPALDA | y | **−0,666** | 1,000 | 1,999 | 2,997 | 0,353 / 2,101 mm |
| MANGA | x | −0,001 | 1,000 | 2,004 | **3,008** | 0,049 / 0,193 mm |
| MANGA | y | −0,509 | 1,000 | 2,023 | 3,041 | 0,465 / 1,221 mm |

Read as a garment: **the body does not grade in width between XS and S at all, and its width
step is halved at XL** (2,5 where the length does 3) — a real and common grading decision. The
sleeve does not follow the body: its width goes the full 3.

🔑 **This is the inter-size law, and it is precisely the thing 16 measurements could not
supply.** It is also why rank 2 had exactly zero leak in F6.2 while buying nothing: rank 2 is
the *right dimension* — two schedules — but with the two directions left free, the POM targets
never pinned which two.

⚠️ **And the law is not exact.** The separable model leaves 0,33–0,56 mm rms and up to 2,3 mm
on individual points. It is a good description of the RUL, not a substitute for it. Anything
built on the schedule inherits that error; anything built on the table itself does not (§3).

## 6 · The proposal for DECISIONS — for Agus's formal ratification

The brief asked for the wording. Here it is, with the evidence each clause rests on.

> **D-INV-8 · The character criterion of the grading motor.**
>
> 1. **Where a RUL exists, the criterion is «reproduce the RUL».** The motor's grading is
>    correct when, applied to the base size, it returns the pattern-maker's own graded
>    geometry to the precision of the CAD export. Measured on the 837: **0,0073 mm worst over
>    7 376 points**, against an export rounding of 0,0071 mm. This is verifiable, it is a
>    property of an artefact we can hold, and it is the only criterion that asks the motor to
>    reproduce a decision rather than to guess one.
> 2. **Where no RUL exists, the criterion is the CONTRACT, and it is the phase gate.** Every
>    POM back at the target the fitxa declares, to ≤ 0,1 mm. This is what the product promises
>    — geometry that measures what the fitxa says — and it has been met by seven orders of
>    magnitude in every mode of F6.1, F6.2 and F6.3.
> 3. 🚨 **The 0,5 mm per-vertex criterion is RETIRED as a phase gate.** It asked the motor to
>    reproduce a pattern-maker's per-point decisions from 16 measurements. Three sprints have
>    measured that it cannot, from four independent directions — a different penalty (worse),
>    fewer unknowns by coupling (neutral), more targets (21 %, then flat), and now the RUL
>    itself, which shows the missing information is an inter-size law that 16 numbers do not
>    contain. It stays as a **reported diagnostic**, never as a gate.

**Why clause 3 is now a measurement and not a concession.** F6.1 established that Montse's own
field satisfies our constraints to 0,32 mm — the right answer was always inside the feasible
set, so the gap was never freedom or smoothness. F6.3 says what it was instead: the law by
which sizes progress, which on this garment differs **per axis and per piece** (§5.3) and is
simply not recoverable from POM targets that only ever constrain distances.

## 7 · What this opens

1. **The RUL path is now the primary one, and it is cheap.** Reading a RUL and applying it is
   `rul_reader` + a map derived from the `# n` the DXF already carries. No solving, no
   regulariser, no character to defend. Where a customer's CAD gives us the table, the motor
   should apply it and the solver should not be involved at all.
2. 🚩 **What the motor still owes is the sewing line.** The RUL grades the cut contour only
   (§3.1) and the CAD derives the rest. Reproducing a graded pattern end to end therefore
   needs the offset construction, not more grading.
3. 🚩 **Ask Montse two things, in one message.** (a) Rules **2–15** are declared and invoked by
   nothing in this DXF — do they belong to pieces of the style we were not sent? (b) The body's
   width step is **halved at XL** (2,5 where the length is 3) while the sleeve's is not: is that
   deliberate? It is the same kind of question the **D** raised, and the same kind F6.2 raised
   about the front's three XS/M ratios — which, note, §5.2 now explains as a fact about the
   garment rather than a fault in the fitxa.
4. 🚩 **`solve_declared` needs a regulariser with an opinion about magnitude before its
   number means anything.** §4.3 measures the defect precisely: 85 unpinned degrees of freedom
   and an energy that is blind to a large affine field. The bounded fix is a non-zero `stretch`
   or a norm penalty on the coefficients, re-measured against the same bank. It is **not** done
   here on purpose — tuning a regulariser until a metric improves, on the bench that metric is
   reported from, is how a motor acquires a character nobody chose. The mode is built, tested,
   and keeps the contract absolutely; what it does not yet have is a defensible prior.
5. **A schedule is still worth having as an input.** Two numbers per axis against 75 rules is a
   far weaker ask, and a customer who cannot export a RUL may well be able to state how their
   sizes progress.
6. **`vora` (S, S2) is still unimplemented**, unchanged from F6.2: what is missing is the
   arc-length measure and its derivative, not the data.
